# Focus group: Village Savings Circle (fictitious)

MODERATOR: Thank you all for coming. Before we start, how is everyone doing today?

GRACE OKAFOR: Fine, thank you. The road from Marlow Creek was muddy, so I am a little late.

DANIEL MENSAH: Good, good. I left my brother Samuel minding the stall.

RUTH ADEYEMI: I am well. My daughter is with a neighbour, so I can stay the whole hour.

MODERATOR: Let us talk about the savings group. What do you use the money you save for?

GRACE OKAFOR: Mostly school fees. In September I paid for both children out of my share, without borrowing.

HANNAH BOATENG: Last year my mother fell ill and the emergency fund paid for the clinic. Without it I would have sold the goats.

JOSEPH TETTEH: I am saving to buy a second sewing machine for the workshop.

MODERATOR: What makes you trust, or not trust, how the group handles the money?

RUTH ADEYEMI: The treasurer reads the accounts aloud every week and each of us signs the ledger.

DANIEL MENSAH: In another group a woman left with the cash box. Here the box has three padlocks and three different people keep the keys.

HANNAH BOATENG: I trust it because I get a receipt every time. If anyone doubts, they can call the treasurer on 020 7946 0142 and she shows the book.

JOSEPH TETTEH: Honestly, I was not sure at first. I asked to see the ledger twice before I believed it.

MODERATOR: Is it ever hard to pay the weekly contribution?

GRACE OKAFOR: Yes. My husband works the harvest and is only paid when the season ends.

DANIEL MENSAH: At the stall some weeks I sell a lot and some weeks nothing. In the rains nobody comes to market.

JOSEPH TETTEH: Orders for uniforms come in January, and then it is quiet until September.

RUTH ADEYEMI: I do casual work, washing and cleaning, so I never know on Monday what I will have on Friday.

MODERATOR: How does your family react to you saving?

HANNAH BOATENG: My mother-in-law keeps saying the money would be better spent on the house now.

GRACE OKAFOR: My husband wanted me to take it all out for his cousin's wedding. I said no.

RUTH ADEYEMI: My children already know not to ask. But my sister asks every month for a loan from my share.

DANIEL MENSAH: At home they tell me I am foolish to hand money to strangers.

MODERATOR: What do you think of the group's rules?

JOSEPH TETTEH: The fine for arriving late is fair. Without it nobody would come on time.

HANNAH BOATENG: The meeting time is difficult for me because I am at the market until four. We asked to move it.

GRACE OKAFOR: The rules are written down and every new member signs them.

DANIEL MENSAH: I think the fine for missing a payment is too high when you have had a bad week.

MODERATOR: Is there anything else you would like to add?

RUTH ADEYEMI: Only that I would like the group to keep going next year.

JOSEPH TETTEH: If you need the list of members for your report, write to me at joseph.tetteh@example.org.

HANNAH BOATENG: Nothing more, thank you. Perhaps hold another meeting like this one.

GRACE OKAFOR: Thank you for listening to us.
